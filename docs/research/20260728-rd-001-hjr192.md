## 1. What HJR-192 actually did

House Joint Resolution 192, 73rd Congress, Public Resolution No. 10, approved
**June 5, 1933**, **48 Stat. 112**. Three operative clauses:

- every provision purporting to give the obligee a right to require payment in gold
  "is declared to be against public policy"
- every obligation "shall be discharged upon payment, dollar for dollar, in any coin
  or currency which at the time of payment is legal tender"
- U.S. coins and currency "shall be legal tender for all debts, public and private,
  public charges, taxes, duties, and dues"

That is the whole of it: it voided **gold-payment clauses** in contracts and confirmed
ordinary currency as valid payment. It created no debt-discharge mechanism, no refund
right, and no account belonging to individuals.

Text: <https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gold_Repeal_Joint_Resolution>
Codification: <https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section5118>

## 2. Current legal status

Codified at **31 U.S.C. § 5118**. Subsection 5118(d)(2) carries the discharge-on-payment
language but expressly "does not apply to an obligation issued after October 27, 1977" —
the Act of Oct. 28, 1977, **Pub. L. 95-147**, restored parties' freedom to write
enforceable gold clauses into new contracts.

- <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5118>
- **Norman v. Baltimore & Ohio R. Co., 294 U.S. 240 (1935)** — upheld the 1933
  resolution as applied to private contracts
- **Perry v. United States, 294 U.S. 330 (1935)** — Congress could not void the gold
  clause in the government's own bonds; plaintiff nonetheless denied damages

HJR-192 was never a bankruptcy filing, a declaration of national insolvency, or a
discharge mechanism for individuals. No source supports that reading.

## 6. The misquoted language, verbatim

| Quoted phrase | What it actually means |
|---|---|
| "…is declared to be against public policy" | Voids only contract terms requiring payment **specifically in gold**. Misused to imply the whole debt system was invalidated. |
| "shall be discharged upon payment, dollar for dollar, in any coin or currency which at the time of payment is legal tender" | A debt is satisfied once **actually paid** in legal tender. Misused to claim a form filing discharges debt with no payment. |
| "legal tender for all debts, public and private, public charges, taxes, duties, and dues" | Defines the valid **medium** of payment. Misused to suggest currency is a drawable government account. |
| **"the United States declared bankruptcy"** | **Appears nowhere in HJR-192.** Fabricated framing layered on the real text. |

## 7. What the authorities call it

IRS: **"redemption" / "commercial redemption" theory** (Rev. Rul. 2004-31);
**"straw man" theory** (Rev. Rul. 2005-21). DOJ Tax Division: **"1099-OID"** or
**"OID redemption"** scheme. DOJ has labeled a defendant a **"sovereign citizen"** in a
press-release title. Related fictitious-instrument case law cited in IRS guidance:
*United States v. Anderson*, 353 F.3d 490, 500 (6th Cir. 2003); *United States v.
Johnson*, 795 F.3d 840 (8th Cir. 2015).
